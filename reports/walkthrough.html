ballpark.

A cricket project I built with public data. What it does, and why the numbers are put together the way they are.

the problem

The scorecard hides two things.

The situation. A six in the 3rd over of a flat chase isn't a six in the 19th defending nine an over. Average and strike rate can't tell them apart. Small samples. A death strike rate off 40 balls is barely a reading. Slice the data fine enough to be useful and it's mostly noise. Nearly every question worth asking about a player runs into one or both.

the data

Public ball-by-ball data. Nothing more.

0

295,732 deliveries · 1,243 IPL matches · 2008 to 2026 · from cricsheet.org. No ball tracking, no fielding positions, no pitch maps. I wasn't trying to out-data a broadcaster. I wanted the method to be right.

layer 1 · expected runs

What's an average ball worth, right here?

For this over, these wickets in hand, this required rate, this batter twenty balls in — what does an average batter score off this ball? That's expected runs. It doesn't know who's batting, and that's the point. Every player number after this is measured against it.

layer 2 · win probability

The odds, every ball.

The win-probability line — a whole match in one picture. And it holds up: across every ball in the data, when it says 70%, the side goes on to win about 70% of the time. It's off by about 2 points on average.

layer 3 · player impact

40 balls is not a sample.

± 13
after shrinkage
± 8

A raw "runs above average" for a short career is mostly wobble. So every number gets pulled back toward the pack — hard when there's little data, barely at all for a long career. It's a ridge regression, which is really just a prior on every player.

His raw economy looks ordinary. But he bowls the 18th and 20th to set batters. Account for who and when, and he's the best bowler in the data — 95% range, +30 to +42.

layer 4 · matchups

"He can't play left-arm spin."

39
19%

Across 692 batter-vs-bowling-type pairings, the average raw gap from expected is 39 runs per 100 balls. Regress it for how thin it is and it's 7.5, about a fifth. The type-level stuff is real. The personal "he can't play it" mostly isn't, once you count the balls.

layer 4 · tactics

Which bowler, which over?

captain
optimiser

An optimiser tries every legal way to bowl out the rest of a chase and picks the toughest one for the batting side. On 1,180 real tight finishes its plan lands within a couple of points of the captain's 84% of the time. Captains mostly get it right — the biggest gap in the data is about 18 points.

the app

Six tabs you can click through.

Match replay Players Matchups Tactics Model card Match simulator

Match replay, player leaderboards, the matchup explorer, the optimiser, and a model card that says where the models fall short — which not many cricket models do.

the simulator

Or bring your own match.

Load any Cricsheet T20 zip, set two line-ups, and it plays the game out ball by ball, all in your browser. Recent form weighted toward strong opposition, a pace-vs-spin matchup, who's set, how the last few overs have gone, and a calibrated win probability at the end.
