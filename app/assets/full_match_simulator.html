Full Match Simulator

Both teams field a batting line-up and a bowling plan. Each side's innings is simulated ball by ball; every event probability is shaped by the batter, the specific bowler and their pace/spin type, the phase and batting position, the venue, wickets in hand, how set the batter is, and in-innings momentum. Win probability blends a standalone-innings score comparison with a required-rate-aware chase pass, then calibrates for how much data the model has.

    3. Automatic model

    Every batter's and bowler's profile is a weighted pool of their own last 15 innings/matches (most recent first), blended with the league-wide phase/position pattern and tilted by the venue's own history. On top of that, five match-state effects are fit automatically the same empirical-Bayes way: wickets in hand (over-conditioned, a Duckworth–Lewis-style resource tilt), how set the batter is (balls faced this innings), a batter × pace/spin match-up (bowler type inferred from where they bowl), in-innings momentum (run rate off the last 12 balls), and, for the win probability only, a second-innings chase pressure pass. Every weight is chosen by walk-forward cross-validation, an Elo-based opponent-strength model, and shrinkage toward the league baseline — nothing here is hand-typed, and the numbers below are read-only, computed once per data load.

    5. Venue factor

    How has this ground played recently, compared to the overall league? The same weighted-pooling used for batters/bowlers is applied to the venue's own last 15 innings (any team, any batter) and compared to the all-venues baseline, then shrunk toward "average venue" using the automatic shrinkage constant shown in the Model panel above — a venue with little recorded history pulls close to neutral, one with a long track record keeps nearly all of its own signal. A ratio above 1× means that event happens more often at this ground than average.

    6. Run simulation

    For any ball, bowler-to-batter probability = (batter's vector + bowler's vector) / 2, blended toward the league-wide phase/position norm and tilted by the venue factor above — the automatically-fit blend weights from the Model panel are applied the same way to every ball, for both innings. Each bowler still bowls exactly the overs you allotted, but which over each spell lands in is drawn fresh every simulation (no two overs in a row from the same bowler), so the review below shows a different over-by-over plan each run.

    8. Model & feature explanation

    How this prediction was actually produced, and how much each mechanism actually mattered for this specific matchup — measured, not asserted.

    Recency-weighted empirical-Bayes match-state simulation, with Elo-based opponent adjustment

    There is no trained classifier or neural network here — every number the simulation uses is a weighted statistical estimate read directly off the ball-by-ball history you loaded, and every one of those weights is itself chosen automatically, not hand-typed. A chronological Elo rating propagates team strength so a player's history against weak opposition counts less; walk-forward cross-validation picks the recency-decay rate and the phase/position blend that actually predict best; empirical-Bayes shrinkage pulls small-sample venues, players and match-state cells back toward the league baseline in proportion to how little evidence they have; and Monte Carlo simulation plays the match out ball by ball hundreds of times for a full distribution, not a point estimate. On top of the raw batter/bowler profiles the ball probability is tilted, in order, by the pace/spin match-up, the venue, wickets in hand (over-conditioned), how set the batter is, and in-innings momentum — each a shrunk ratio fit the same way. The headline win probability additionally runs a chase pass (each side chasing the other's total under required-rate pressure), blends it with the standalone-innings comparison, and regresses toward 50% when the whole model rests on little data. Every mechanism can be switched off in the feature-importance panel below to see exactly what it is worth for this matchup, or dialled part-way — between its fitted strength and no effect at all — from the ⚙ settings icon, top right.

    Feature importance for this matchup

    Computed by ablation: the exact same matchup is re-simulated with one mechanism at a time switched off (same random seed, so only that mechanism's effect differs), and the resulting swing in predicted combined runs and win probability is measured directly — not a formula, an actual rerun.

    Engineered features

    Every input the model consumes, computed live from the loaded data for this matchup's top-order batters.

    Every batter's vector is their own last 15 innings, recency-weighted by an automatically-fit decay rate and reweighted per-sample by how strong the opponent was at the time (an Elo model that only ever looks at form before that match), with missing history filled from the league-wide average at their batting position; every bowler's is built the same way. The venue's own vector is pooled the same way and shrunk toward the all-venues baseline. On top of that, five shrunk match-state ratios tilt each ball's probability: the batter × pace/spin match-up, wickets in hand (over-conditioned), how set the batter is, in-innings momentum, and — for the win probability only — a second-innings chase pass under required-rate pressure, blended with the standalone-innings score comparison and regressed toward 50% by how much data the model rests on. Every one of these numbers is fit from the data itself, with no manually-tuned weight anywhere in the pipeline. Nothing here is hidden: every simulated match is retained ball by ball, both innings, and every mechanism can be ablated to see its effect.
